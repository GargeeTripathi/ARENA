# Northbridge Institute of Technology — Student Handbook 2026–27

## 1. About the Institute

The Northbridge Institute of Technology (NIT-B) was founded in 1987 in the lakeside town of Varkala Ridge. It began as a polytechnic with three departments: Civil Engineering, Electrical Engineering and Applied Chemistry. In 2004 it was granted autonomous status by the Regional Board of Technical Education, which allows it to design its own curriculum and conduct its own examinations. The Institute today has eleven departments, roughly 6,400 students and 410 full-time faculty members. The campus covers 212 acres and includes a 40-acre wetland reserve that is maintained jointly with the Varkala Ridge Ecological Trust.

The current Director is Prof. Meenakshi Raghavan, who took office in July 2023. The Dean of Student Affairs is Dr. Tobias Okonkwo-Lindqvist. Students should address academic grievances first to their department's Faculty Advisor, then to the Dean of Academics, and only then to the Director's office.

The Institute's motto is "Measure twice, build once." The official colours are slate blue and amber.

## 2. Academic Calendar

The academic year is divided into two regular semesters and an optional summer term.

The Monsoon Semester runs from 4 August to 12 December. The Spring Semester runs from 5 January to 30 April. The Summer Term is a seven-week intensive session that begins on 18 May and is restricted to backlog courses and approved minor-credit electives.

Mid-semester examinations are held in the eighth week of each regular semester. End-semester examinations occupy the final two weeks. Results are published within 21 calendar days of the last examination.

Registration for each semester closes nine days after classes begin. A student who has not registered by that date must apply for late registration, which is allowed only up to the end of the third week and carries a late fee (see Section 4).

## 3. Attendance Policy

Attendance is recorded in every lecture, tutorial and laboratory session. A student must maintain at least 78 percent attendance in each course to be eligible to sit the end-semester examination in that course. Medical leave supported by a certificate from the Campus Health Centre may be counted towards attendance, up to a maximum of 10 percent of the total sessions in a course.

A student whose attendance falls between 70 and 78 percent may appeal to the Attendance Review Committee, which meets in the eleventh week of each semester. Appeals must be filed through the student portal at least five working days before the committee meets. Attendance below 70 percent cannot be condoned under any circumstances, and the student receives a grade of "XA" (debarred for attendance) in that course.

Laboratory courses have a stricter rule: a student who misses more than two laboratory sessions without a medical certificate must complete make-up sessions, which are scheduled on Saturdays and cost a fee per session.

## 5. Grading System

NIT-B uses a ten-point grading scale. The letter grades and their grade points are: O (10), A+ (9), A (8), B+ (7), B (6), C (5), P (4) and F (0). A grade of P is the minimum passing grade. The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) is the credit-weighted mean of the grade points earned in that semester. The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is the credit-weighted mean over all semesters.

A student who receives an F may re-register for the course in a later semester or in the Summer Term. The higher of the two grades is used in CGPA calculations, but both attempts appear on the transcript.

To graduate with distinction, a B.Tech student must earn a CGPA of at least 8.75 with no F grades at any point. A student on academic probation — defined as an SGPA below 5.0 in any semester — may register for no more than 18 credits in the following semester.

## 6. Hostel Rules

Hostel residents must return to their hostel by 10:30 PM on weekdays and 11:30 PM on Fridays and Saturdays. Guests are allowed in the common lounge between 9:00 AM and 7:00 PM only. Cooking appliances, including induction plates and electric kettles above 1,000 watts, are not permitted in rooms.

Hostel allocation for first-year students is done by lottery. Senior students are allocated rooms according to their CGPA, with ties broken by attendance percentage. A student who violates hostel rules three times in one semester may be asked to vacate the hostel, with no refund of the hostel fee for that semester.

## 7. Library

The Central Library, named after the Institute's founder, Dr. Hari Prasad Venkatesan, holds about 1,85,000 printed volumes and provides access to 34 online journal databases. Undergraduate students may borrow up to six books at a time for 14 days; postgraduate students may borrow up to ten books for 30 days. The overdue charge is INR 5 per book per day, capped at INR 500 per book. The library is open from 8:00 AM to midnight on weekdays and from 9:00 AM to 9:00 PM on weekends. During the end-semester examination period it remains open 24 hours.

## 8. Academic Integrity

Plagiarism, unauthorised collaboration and the use of generative AI tools in assessments where they are not explicitly permitted are treated as academic misconduct. A first offence results in a zero for the assessment concerned. A second offence results in an F grade in the course. A third offence is referred to the Disciplinary Board, which may suspend the student for up to two semesters.
