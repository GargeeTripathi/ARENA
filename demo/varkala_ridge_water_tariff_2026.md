# Varkala Ridge Municipal Council — Water Supply Tariff and Regulations 2026–27

Approved by Council Resolution No. 117/2026 on 28 February 2026. Effective from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. Issued by the Water Works Department, Municipal Office, Lake Road.

## 1. Introduction

The Varkala Ridge Municipal Council supplies treated water to about 38,000 households and 2,100 commercial connections. Raw water is drawn from Lake Kayalkara and treated at the Ridge Treatment Plant, which has a capacity of 42 million litres per day. Treated water is tested daily at the plant and weekly at 60 sampling points across the town. The council's target is to keep total dissolved solids (TDS) of supplied water below 450 ppm. In the dry months of March to May, TDS in the eastern wards can rise to about 700 ppm because water is drawn from deeper wells to supplement the lake.

## 2. Supply Hours

Water is supplied twice daily in all wards: from 5:30 AM to 8:30 AM and from 6:00 PM to 8:00 PM. Wards 14 to 19, which are at higher elevation, receive water in the morning slot only, from 5:00 AM to 9:00 AM, during the dry months. Planned interruptions are announced at least 48 hours in advance through the council's SMS service and local newspapers.

## 3. Domestic Tariff

Domestic connections are billed every two months. The charge depends on the volume used in the billing period, measured in kilolitres (1 kilolitre = 1,000 litres).

| Consumption slab (kilolitres per two months) | Rate (INR per kilolitre) |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10 | 4.50 |
| 10 to 30 | 9.00 |
| 30 to 50 | 16.00 |
| above 50 | 28.00 |

In addition, every domestic connection pays a fixed meter charge of INR 60 per bill. Households with a below-poverty-line (BPL) card receive the first 10 kilolitres free of charge in each billing period.

## 4. Commercial and Institutional Tariff

Commercial connections, including hotels, restaurants and shops, are billed monthly at a flat rate of INR 32 per kilolitre, plus a meter charge of INR 250 per bill. Educational institutions, hospitals and places of worship pay a concessional rate of INR 14 per kilolitre. The Northbridge Institute of Technology is billed under the institutional category and has a separate bulk supply agreement for its hostels.

## 5. New Connections

| Item | Charge (INR) |
|---|---|
| Domestic connection fee | 3,500 |
| Commercial connection fee | 12,000 |
| Road cutting and restoration (per metre) | 850 |
| Meter (if supplied by the council) | 2,200 |
| Security deposit, domestic | 1,000 |
| Security deposit, commercial | 5,000 |

Applications are processed within 21 working days. A connection is sanctioned only if the property has a registered building plan and a sump or storage tank of at least 1,000 litres.

## 6. Payment, Late Fees and Disconnection

Bills must be paid within 21 days of the bill date. A late payment surcharge of 2 percent per month is added on unpaid amounts. If a bill remains unpaid for 90 days, the council may disconnect the supply after giving 15 days' written notice. Reconnection costs INR 500 for domestic and INR 2,000 for commercial connections, after all dues are paid.

Bills can be paid online on the council website, at the Municipal Office, or at any of the 12 "Jal Seva" kiosks. A rebate of 1 percent is given on domestic bills paid online within 7 days of the bill date.

## 7. Water Quality Complaints

Residents who notice discoloured or foul-smelling water should call the Water Works helpline at 0472-266-1180. The department must collect a sample within 24 hours and share test results within 72 hours. If the water is found unfit for drinking, the council supplies drinking water by tanker free of charge until the problem is fixed.

The council recommends household purification for residents in the eastern wards during the dry months. Domestic purifiers rated for TDS up to 2,000 ppm are adequate for municipal supply in all wards.

## 8. Conservation Rules

Using a hose to wash vehicles or pavements with municipal water is not permitted between 1 March and 31 May. The fine is INR 1,000 for a first offence and INR 5,000 for a repeat offence. Rainwater harvesting is mandatory for all new buildings with a roof area above 100 square metres; buildings that comply receive a 5 percent rebate on the property tax.
